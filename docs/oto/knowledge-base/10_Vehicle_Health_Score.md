# Vehicle Health Score

## Overview

Every vehicle on Otopair has a Vehicle Health Score from **0 to 100**. It grades the car's upkeep — how well its maintenance is keeping up — never the owner. It's upkeep, not a diagnosis, and it isn't a safety rating.

## What the score tracks

A short list of systems: **engine oil, brakes, tires and the 12-volt battery**, plus the **state inspection** when a record of it is on file. Each item is graded as on time, due soon, needs attention or overdue, from the car's service intervals, what shops measure, and the mileage the driver reports.

It does **not** track a hybrid or EV drive battery, the transmission, suspension, air conditioning or anything else — it isn't a whole-car check. Where the app has no record of something, it treats it as unchecked, not as healthy.

## What moves it

- **A due service gets done** — the booking completes, or the driver logs one done elsewhere, and the item goes back to on time
- **A warning light** — an active dashboard warning light lowers the score while it's on, and clearing it removes the deduction. The oil pressure and temperature lights count the most
- **A mechanic's open recommendation** — lowers it gradually, phased in so the score never drops suddenly
- **A shop inspects the car** — the mechanic's measurements land on the record shortly after the visit closes. A problem fixed in the same visit never lowers the score
- **The quarterly check-in** — answering keeps the score current; skip it and the score is shown as an estimate until the driver answers

## What does NOT move it

- App inactivity — the score is never reduced because someone hasn't opened the app
- Rewards, referrals, Health Points or anything bought — there is no way to buy a higher score
- Connected-car data — nothing is read from the car. No telematics, no connected-car feed

## Where the data comes from

Service records, quarterly check-in answers, the warning lights the driver reports, and what a mechanic measures during an inspection. Because nothing is read from the car, the score can't see a fault the car hasn't shown and the driver hasn't reported.

## What the score is not

- **Not a safety rating.** It doesn't test crashworthiness or inspect anything it doesn't track, and a high score is not a statement that the car is safe to drive
- A lit warning light or a stop-driving instruction always outranks the score

## Core principles

- **Predictive, not reactive** — surfaces what's coming up, not just what's overdue
- **Calm, not alarming** — no red panic states unless truly critical
- **Informational, not judgmental** — surfaces facts, never blames the owner
- **Always paired with clear next steps** — never just a score, always a "here's what to do"
- **No sudden drops without explanation** — every change is contextualized

## What completing a service does

When the driver completes a service that was due, the item goes back to on time and the score gains back what that item had lost. The app shows a clear **+X** next to the item so the driver can see the connection between the service and the score.

## Language guidance

Always use calm, protective language:
- ✅ "Service recommended" not "Service overdue"
- ✅ "Protection declining" not "Vehicle at risk"
- ✅ "Overdue by 500 miles" not "YOU'RE OVERDUE!"
- ✅ "Here's what to do next" — pair every status with a concrete action

## How to answer common questions

**"What does my health score mean?"**
"It's a 0-to-100 grade of your car's upkeep — oil, brakes, tires, the 12-volt battery and your state inspection. The app pairs it with the next step to take. It grades the car's upkeep, not you, and it isn't a safety rating."

**"My score dropped — what happened?"**
"Usually one of three things: something it tracks came due with time or miles, a warning light is on, or a mechanic left an open recommendation. The app shows which item moved and what to do next."

**"Is the health score a safety rating?"**
"No. It grades upkeep. A high score doesn't mean the car is safe to drive, and a warning light always outranks it."

**"Does it connect to my car?"**
"No. Nothing is read from the car — no telematics. It's built from your service records, your check-in answers, the warning lights you report and what a mechanic measures."

**"Does ignoring the app hurt my score?"**
"No. Opening the app or not doesn't change it — only real upkeep does. If you skip the quarterly check-in, the score just shows as an estimate until you answer."

**"Can I pay to raise my score?"**
"No. Only real upkeep moves it."

## Important boundaries

- Do **not** use alarmist language ("danger," "failure," "critical risk") unless the situation is genuinely safety-critical
- Do **not** imply the score judges the owner
- Do **not** call the score a safety rating, or say a high score means the car is safe
- Do **not** say the score reads data from the car
- Do **not** quote internal calculation modifiers (age tier, mileage tier, driving pattern multipliers, etc.) — those are internal mechanics
- Do **not** suggest the score is a competition or a leaderboard
- The right tone is **calm and protective**, never urgent or shaming

02Why "shop rates differ" can't touch our data
You're right that price varies shop to shop. That's exactly why we never store price.
shop labor $  =  labor_hourswe store this — a property of the car  ×  shop_door_ratethe shop sets this — we never touch it

Hours are the flat-rate standard every system (MOTOR · Mitchell1 · ALLDATA · RepairPal) publishes before any shop applies its own rate. They depend on the chassis, the engine, and how the part mounts — not on who's quoting. The shop-to-shop variance you describe lives entirely in rate, which is computed at read time and stays the shop's. The inconsistency literally cannot enter our data — by construction.

05"If it's off — how off is it?"

Hours track physics, not rates

Spark-plug hours must scale with how hard the plugs are to reach. They do — exactly. A rate-driven number would scatter randomly.

CR-V · I40.6h
Atlas · V62.0h
M550i · V83.3h

RepairPal gives time, not price

The high÷low dollar ratio is a constant ≈1.47 across every service & car — only possible if the rate range is fixed and hours is the one variable.

hours = (low+high)/2 ÷ $130 → reproduces the published table.

Same chassis → same hours

The direct disproof of "massive inconsistency." M550i brake pads = 1.51 h, taken from its 530i G30 sibling. Rate-driven data scatters across shops; time clusters by chassis.

And it self-corrects: once a config logs ≥3 real single-service job_actuals, the empirical median overrides the book estimate — live.

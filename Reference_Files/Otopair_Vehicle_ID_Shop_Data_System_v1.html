Vehicle ID & Shop Data System — v1

The persistent vehicle passport that travels with every car across every shop interaction. Includes the pre-job confirmation flow, post-job data collection, data hierarchy rules, and UI placement recommendations. Combined spec for Tasks 2 & 3.
Status: Spec draft — for team review  ·  Version: 1.0  ·  Date: April 2026  ·  Source: Team meeting April 18, 2026
Section 1
What Is the Vehicle ID?
A persistent, shop-facing data profile attached to every vehicle in the Otopair system. It's the technical "passport" that a mechanic sees — not what the user sees. Think of it as the vehicle's driver's license: when a car arrives at any partner shop, the shop already knows everything about it.
The Analogy
Driver's license for the car

When you walk into a bar, your ID tells them your name, your age, your address — they don't ask you to fill out a form. Same concept: when a car arrives at any Otopair partner shop, the shop sees the vehicle's ID — everything they need, pre-filled and ready.

The Power
Every shop already knows your car

The mechanic doesn't ask "what oil do you use?" — they already see it. They don't guess the tire size — it's right there. They don't wonder if the brakes were done recently — the service history shows it. And it gets smarter with every visit.

The Vehicle ID is shop-facing only. The user sees their clean, minimal vehicle card in the app. The Vehicle ID is the detailed, technical view that the shop sees — written in shop language, structured for shop workflows.
Section 2
Two Layers of Data
The Vehicle ID has two distinct layers — what we know from day one, and what we learn over time.
Layer 1 — Static
Rarely changes. Known from onboarding.

Year, make, model, trim, engine type, displacement, drivetrain, VIN (if provided). This data comes from the vehicle itself and is populated during user onboarding, enriched by Waleed's vehicle data system (wheelsize.com API + fallback defaults).

Source: User onboarding + AI enrichment

Editable by shop: No (read-only — this is factory data)

Layer 2 — Living
Changes over time. Built through service interactions.

Current tires (brand, model, size), brake pad brand, type, and condition (front and rear), rotor condition, oil type and viscosity, brake fluid type, coolant type, current mileage, last service date, mechanic notes, aftermarket modifications. This data starts empty and fills up through shop reports, user input, and service history.

Source: Shop reports (primary), user input (secondary), AI enrichment (fallback)

Editable by shop: Yes — shops confirm or update at every visit

Section 4
Data Hierarchy — Who Do We Trust?
When multiple sources provide conflicting data, the system needs a clear priority order. This was agreed in the April 18 team meeting.
Priority 1 — Shop-Reported Data (Source of Truth)

If a shop has confirmed or entered data, it's the most reliable. They physically looked at the car. If the same data point has been confirmed by a shop 2-3 times consecutively, it's marked as Verified and the system stops asking for confirmation on that field.

Priority 2 — User-Provided Data

Information the user entered during onboarding or through photo capture. Useful for initial population but always superseded by shop data if they conflict. Marked as Unverified until a shop confirms it.

Priority 3 — AI-Enriched Data (Fallback)

OEM specifications from Waleed's vehicle data system — what the car should have based on year/make/model/trim. Correct for factory specs but may not reflect the car's current state (owner may have changed oil type, aftermarket parts, etc.). Used as a starting point until real data is collected.

Section 5
Vehicle ID Lifecycle — How It Grows
The Vehicle ID starts thin and gets richer with every interaction. Here's the full lifecycle from vehicle creation to a mature, verified profile.
1

User adds vehicle during onboarding

Layer 1 (static) populates automatically — year, make, model, trim. AI enrichment fills OEM specs — engine type, drivetrain, factory tire size, oil viscosity. User optionally provides VIN, last service info, driving habits. Layer 2 (living) is mostly empty except for user-provided onboarding answers.

Vehicle ID status: Partial. Layer 1 complete. Layer 2 mostly AI defaults with "Unverified" tags.
2

First shop visit — pre-job confirmation

The car arrives at a partner shop for any service. The shop sees the Vehicle ID with whatever data exists. Layer 1 is filled. Layer 2 has AI defaults and user data. The shop confirms what's there and fills in what's missing — they physically look at the car and report: actual tires on it, oil viscosity sticker, current mileage. This is the pre-job form.

Vehicle ID status: Substantially populated. First real-world data layer. Many fields move from "Unverified" to shop-confirmed.
3

First shop visit — post-job update

After completing the service, the shop reports what changed. New oil type installed, new parts used, mechanic notes. The Vehicle ID updates — "Oil changed to Castrol Edge 0W-20 on March 15 at 47,230 mi." First mechanic note added: "Customer mentioned slight vibration above 60mph — worth checking wheel balance next visit."

Vehicle ID status: Rich. Service history started. Living data layer has its first real entries.
4

Subsequent visits — progressive enrichment

Every future visit to any partner shop, the shop sees the Vehicle ID pre-filled. They confirm "no changes" on verified fields or update what's different. Mileage updates every visit. Service history grows. Mechanic notes accumulate. After 2-3 consistent confirmations, fields lock as "Verified" and the system stops asking about them.

Vehicle ID status: Mature and verified. The system knows this car deeply. Every shop on the network benefits from every previous shop's data.
The Retention Loop
Leaving Otopair means losing all this accumulated intelligence

The more the car is serviced through Otopair, the richer the Vehicle ID becomes. A car with 8 service visits through the platform has a comprehensive profile — tire history, fluid preferences, mechanic notes, mileage trajectory, service timing. That data doesn't exist anywhere else. This is a natural retention mechanism that gets stronger with every interaction.

Section 6
Pre-Job Confirmation Form
What the shop sees and confirms when a car arrives for service. This is the mechanism that creates and updates the Vehicle ID. It must be fast — under 60 seconds for a return vehicle, under 2 minutes for a first-time vehicle.
Design Principle
Pre-filled, not blank

This is NOT a blank form the shop fills out from scratch. It's a pre-filled summary of everything we already know, presented for confirmation or correction. Existing data shows as pre-populated fields. The shop confirms with one tap ("All correct") or edits individual fields. Empty fields are clearly marked as "Not yet reported" for the shop to fill in.

Pre-Job Form — Field by Field

1

Current Mileage

Numeric input. Pre-filled with last reported mileage if available. Required — always collected at every visit. This drives the entire service interval engine.

2

Tires — Current Specs

Pre-filled with whatever we have (AI default, user photo, or previous shop report). Shop confirms or updates: brand, model, size (front/rear if staggered), run-flat status. Also a quick condition assessment: Good / Fair / Replace Soon. Required on first visit. On subsequent visits, if verified, show as confirmed with option to update.

3

Fluids — Current Specs

Pre-filled with OEM defaults from AI enrichment. Shop confirms or updates: oil viscosity, oil type (conventional/blend/synthetic), coolant type, brake fluid type. Required on first visit. On subsequent visits, if verified, shows as confirmed.

4

Modifications / Notes Check

Pre-filled with any existing mechanic notes or reported modifications. Shop confirms "nothing new" or adds observations. Optional — but valuable. If a previous mechanic noted something, the current mechanic should see it and can confirm or update.

5

"All Correct" Confirmation

Once the shop has reviewed/updated everything, one tap to confirm: "Vehicle specs confirmed." This logs the confirmation with a timestamp and marks fields as shop-verified. On return visits where everything is already verified, this could be the ONLY step — one tap and they're done.

Timor's idea from the meeting: if the same data has been confirmed 2-3 times by any shop, the field auto-locks as "Verified" and the form shows it as a confirmed badge instead of an editable field. The shop can still override if something changed, but they don't need to re-confirm static data every visit. This is how the form gets faster over time — a mature Vehicle ID means a one-tap pre-job confirmation.
Section 7
Post-Job Data Collection
What the shop reports after completing the service. This has two parts: mandatory vehicle data (what changed on the car) and optional optimization data (how accurate was our estimate). From the April 18 meeting.
Part A — Mandatory
Vehicle data: What changed?

This is what updates the Vehicle ID. The shop must report what was done and what's now on the car.

This feeds: Vehicle ID, Vehicle Health score, service history, next service predictions.

Part B — Optional
Optimization data: How was our estimate?

This helps Otopair's system get smarter over time. Was the labor time accurate? Were the parts correct? Any surprises?

This feeds: labor time accuracy, parts database, pricing calibration, spec variance tracking.

Part A — Mandatory Post-Job Questions

Shop MUST complete these before marking a job as complete.

1

Updated mileage

Current mileage at job completion. Pre-filled with pre-job mileage. Shop confirms or updates. Required

2

Parts used

What was installed? Repeatable section: part name, brand, OEM part number (optional), cost. Pre-filled with expected parts from vehicle specs if available. Shop confirms or overrides with what they actually used. Required — at least one entry for non-labor-only services.

3

Service-specific updates to Vehicle ID

Depending on the service performed, relevant Vehicle ID fields auto-prompt for update:

Oil change → "Update oil type/viscosity on Vehicle ID?"
Tire replacement → "Update tire brand/model/size on Vehicle ID?"
Brake service → "Update brake pad brand/part # on Vehicle ID?"
Coolant flush → "Update coolant type on Vehicle ID?"

This is context-aware — the form only asks about fields relevant to the service just performed. Required

4

Mechanic notes (Tip for next mechanic)

Free-text field for observations that should travel with the car. "Drain plug slightly worn." "Customer asked about timing belt — consider recommending at next visit." "Front left tire wearing unevenly — possible alignment issue." Optional — but strongly encouraged. These are the notes that make Otopair's service quality exceptional.

5

Flag for review

Checkbox: "Our vehicle specs for this car may be incorrect." If the mechanic found that the OEM data doesn't match reality (e.g., manual says 0W-20 but car actually needs 5W-30), this flags the vehicle record for admin/Waleed review. Optional

Part B — Optional Post-Job Questions

Helps Otopair optimize data accuracy. Shop can skip these — they won't block job completion.

1

Actual labor time

How long did the job actually take? Numeric input in minutes. Pre-filled with estimated labor time. Feeds variance tracking — if our estimates are consistently off, we adjust.

2

Difficulty rating

1-5 scale. Was this job easier or harder than expected for this vehicle? Helps calibrate labor time estimates for this specific year/make/model.

3

Parts accuracy

Were the parts we suggested correct? Yes / No — if No, what was different? Feeds the vehicle specs database accuracy over time.

4

Anything else to report?

Open text. Catches anything the structured fields didn't cover. "Customer needs to come back for alignment." "Noticed exhaust leak — unrelated to today's service."

From the meeting: Part B is an optional survey. The shop can skip through with "Not available" on any service category they didn't work on and only answer questions relevant to the job they performed. This prevents the form from feeling overwhelming on simple jobs.
Section 8
Where This Lives in the Shop Dashboard
Based on the current shop portal design, here's where the Vehicle ID and forms integrate.
UI Placement 1

Booking Detail Panel — Vehicle Name as Clickable Link

In the current booking detail panel (right sidebar when you click a job), the vehicle name shows as "2018 Ford F-150". This should become a clickable link or button that opens the full Vehicle ID view. The shop can see the vehicle's complete profile without leaving the booking context. Small icon next to the vehicle name — like a document or ID card icon — signals it's tappable.

UI Placement 2

Pre-Job Form — Triggered on "Accept" or "Start Job"

When the shop accepts a booking OR when the mechanic taps "Start Job," the pre-job confirmation form surfaces as a modal or step before work begins. For first-time vehicles, it's the full form (mileage, tires, fluids, notes). For return vehicles with verified data, it could be a one-tap "Vehicle specs confirmed" with the option to view/edit details.

UI Placement 3

Post-Job Form — Triggered on "Complete Job"

This is already scoped in Daniel's Ticket 2.9 (Job Actuals Input Form) and Ticket 2.15 (Mechanic Job Actuals). The Vehicle ID updates are an ADDITION to the existing job actuals form — not a replacement. Part A (mandatory vehicle data) integrates into the existing required fields. Part B (optional optimization) is a new section at the end.

UI Placement 4

Customer/Vehicle Search — Shop Can Look Up Any Vehicle

In the booking list, the "Vehicle" search filter could also double as a way to pull up a Vehicle ID directly. Shop types "BMW 330i" and can see the full profile even outside of a specific booking context. Useful for when a customer calls and the shop wants to check their car's details.

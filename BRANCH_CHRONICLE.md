# Parts, Pricing & Labor era (Jun 8–10)


With the groundwork from the previous chapter in place — the Service Parts Reference resolver (`9025ad0`), the director per-config backfill buttons (`98bf93f`), and the fire-and-return reprice trigger (`ccf0206`) — the branch turned to a three-day sprint with one goal: make every number in a quote defensible. Labor hours got a real source, parts prices got a verification contract, the admin surface got locked down, the pipeline got failure handling, the data got human-proofed, and then the whole catalog got swept. This chapter covers `2d828d2` through `5c789e3`.

### Labor: RepairPal/MOTOR becomes a first-class source

The arc opened with design, not code: a spec for RepairPal/MOTOR as a labor-time source (`2d828d2`), a v2 spec adding sibling resolution — chassis/engine-family routing with an LLM router and a validation cascade (`e009ac8`) — and the implementation plan (`91d8dff`). Implementation then went bottom-up through pure, unit-tested helpers: `weightedMedian` for source-weighted aggregation (`cb32fac`); RepairPal URL building, page parsing, and hours recovery from labor dollars (`9edf021` — hours = mid-$ ÷ ~$130, with a 1.47 high/low ratio guard); and pure service-determinant routing, so engine services match engine-family twins and chassis services match chassis-code twins (`b697820`). Schema followed — `labor_determinant`/`repairpal_slug` on services plus observation provenance (`d103531`) — then the per-service determinant table, the RepairPal slug map, and the stamp mutation (`8c7660c`). A Firecrawl scrape action recovered real hours from live pages (`f9498ca`), aggregation became a weighted median with RepairPal-anchored confidence tiers (`d5137d3` — `repairpal_motor` 0.8, LLM 0.3–0.5, VDB demoted to 0.05), and the enrichment pipeline began writing exact-nameplate RepairPal labor dark behind `LABOR_SOURCE_REPAIRPAL` (`8618947`).

Sibling resolution made niche cars work: `deriveEngineFamily` from engine codes (`29872d1`), the resolver itself — catalog plus LLM router, code-validated and probed against populated pages (`471461c`) — the pipeline resolving each nameplate once with sibling fallback (`e850d53`), and an engine-family fallback to `args.engineCode` for desynced configs (`b04bb3c`). Verified live: the M550i takes engine labor from the 750i and chassis labor from the 530i, and the 750i spark-plug quote moved from 3.5 to 3.3h at 0.9 confidence. Dev visibility came via the `verifyLabor` read query (`0056657`) and provenance fields plus a config-dup audit (`017b94b`).

Then the quote gate was hardened (`8960661`): aggregates stamp `data_quality: 'aggregated'`, `isHighQualityVdb` disqualifies on source (training data, web search, clones), the sibling fallback applies the same gate, and `hours_source` reports real provenance — with the recorded decision to flip `LABOR_SOURCE_REPAIRPAL=on` before any catalog relabor. Jun 10 finished the kit: year-correct RepairPal pages via `repairpalUrlCandidates` plus the booking-UI quality gate (`d3aec25`), `relaborConfig` for catalog-wide labor backfill without an LLM batch (`18a722d`), a data-good validation report (`8d19cd1`) made applicability-aware with `timing_system` as grading basis (`b06685a`), and documented URL-format findings — year pages are real and generation-scoped, with a baseVehicleId upgrade queued (`cbb63e6`).

### Parts: two-tier re-extraction and the war on price poison

Repricing was rebuilt to correct what already exists: the reprice action re-parses each part's stored source URLs (`90746fb`) and overwrites every shop row in place, keyed by part_id+domain (`1c05615`) — first proof on the Jetta, where a brake pad mispriced at $17.48 `online_discount` corrected to $34.97 `sale`, 19/35 rows fixed. A `verifyParts` inspector (`a219f2a`) and the re-extraction plan (`86e0407`) set up the main event: domain-agnostic two-tier price re-extraction (`b597c4b`) — Tier 1 deterministic parse, Tier 2 LLM extract-only with guardrails (reject MSRP/"was"/"You Save"; price below MSRP; OEM match; 0.3×–3× cross-source median band) — plus a poison-exclusion median that keeps `online_discount`/`you_save`/`unverified` rows for audit but bars them from customer pricing. Live on the Jetta: `online_discount` 16 → 0. The outcome contract (`3427406`) then split `fetch_failed` (transient — row left untouched) from `unverified` (page read and testified against the number), so a Firecrawl hiccup can never demote a verified price. And when every row for a locked part is poison, the booking quote no longer bills $0 silently — it carries an explicit `price_unknown` marker for the mechanic dialog (`0cd043d`).

The night's biggest find: fresh enrichments never got itemized prices. Batch 2 only priced parts Batch 1 had found, so a first-pass car discovered all its own parts and priced none of them — masked until now because re-enriched cars were seeded by pre-existing parts. The fix requires a `parts_breakdown` entry for every part the model itself reports and normalizes the breakdown→fitment OEM join (`7d9f01e`); verified on a fresh 2018 Civic — locked roles 8 → 23 priced, 15 `llm_estimate` rows where there had been zero, fill 53% → 82% (`95caf3c`). Resolver-side, unusable fitment groups stopped blocking universal seeds (750i gear oil $0 → $22) and an anchor sanity band killed the cached $49 crush washer — consumables priced over 6× their reference anchor swap to the seed, both washers now $4 (`3849aa4`). The last poison writers, diagnoseVin's four price paths, were routed through the same verification contract, so `online_discount` is no longer written anywhere (`10547e5`), and fill-rate stopped counting poison — `getPricedPartCount` now requires at least one trusted row (`1c0cc54`). A review-backlog sweep fixed quote correctness at the edges: differential service now bills RWD/4WD, not just literal "AWD"; the EV applicability gate became case-insensitive (NHTSA writes "Electric"); and Oto's service list filters through the booking surface's applicability check (`54db127`).

### Security lockdowns

Two passes closed the trusted-caller holes. The Jun-9 batch (`aff697e`) token-gated the director backfill trio server-side — audit actor derived from the session, caller-supplied identity no longer trusted — and internalized `purgeVehicleConfig`, the `runPublic` shims, diagnoseVin's write actions, and `backfillNhtsaVinKeys` (dry-run by default). The Jun-10 sweep (`f13dea5`) did the same for all six `directorConfigActions` config-edit mutations, with the token validated in-transaction and `actorName`/`actorId` args removed entirely, so forgery now fails at the validator.

### Stuck-config failure handling

The fresh-Civic experiment killed two enrichment runs at the 600s action cap — a TLS-dead registry source let Firecrawl burn minutes per page — stranding the config as `'enriching'` behind a 4h lockout. The immediate fix was a scrape time budget: 45s per-fetch aborts and a 210s wall clock, after which the run proceeds to batch with whatever it has (`41c07de`). The structural fix (`56e1d51`) added a transactional `failEnrichmentRun` wired into every exit path plus catch-all wrappers on both poll actions, restoring configs to pending/partial instead of stranding them; batch-2 timeouts now finalize with batch-1 data, `getBatchStatus` retries transient errors instead of killing a paid batch, and heartbeat-based force-unstick lets a director take over any run silent for 15 minutes — exactly the failure the Civic reproduced twice. Adjacent integrity fixes: director re-enrich pins to the triggered config_id, ending duplicate-config proliferation (`7d23490`); the scrape-cache key now includes trim, ending cross-trim contamination and flushing the poisoned cache (`3e6f1b5`); and applicability nulls became final — chain-engined cars stop re-growing timing-belt parts on every re-enrich, with the chain rule extended to the whole timing kit (`1d6d02d`, codegen in `1606a00`).

### Data fixes and verified fields

An audited CLI fixer (`0fdd4b7`) — read-only `engineTimingAudit` plus `fixEngineFields` with audit-log rows — corrected five engines on dev, headlined by two timing misclassifications: the Jetta's EA211 (chain→belt) and the 2003 Accord's J30A4 (chain→belt, 3→6 cylinders). Then the session's second-biggest find: the Jetta's own re-enrich clobbered the human fix, because Batch 1 repeated the misclassification — and with applicability nulls final, a misclassified belt car could *never* recover its belt parts. The answer is `engines.verified_fields` (`ca40fae`): human writers stamp every field they set, the pipeline writer skips verified keys, and Batch-1 output is overridden with verified values before applicability runs. Stamps were made idempotent and the Jetta fix re-applied under protection (`019b7cc`), with a curated known-engine-facts table (EA211 and Honda J-series are belt-driven) as a backstop independent of any one car's data (`ce6b386`). Session notes in `c8d09ad`.

### Coverage tooling

The root PDF is the parts contract, and `3413fde` made it executable: `devOnly/partsCoverage` runs the real resolver per service and grades every PDF role as priced/unpriced/missing, immediately surfacing real gaps — the Jetta's oil-change quote billed no oil, and an apparent brake "axle asymmetry." That asymmetry turned out to be the inspector's own bug: the resolver deliberately defaults positionless dual-axle calls to FRONT for billing safety, and with `positionFilter: "both"` the lossiness claim was formally retracted — true coverage jumped to Civic 26/30, Jetta 26/30, 750i 25/30 (`f6c498c`). Applicability-aware grading (`b06685a`) then made chain-engine timing roles report `not_applicable` instead of counting as gaps, while keeping pollution visible.

### Ops: the sweeps and the scoreboard

With the tools landed, Jun-10 session 3 ran the catalog. The reprice sweep over nine configs took `online_discount` poison from **127 rows to 1** catalog-wide — the lone survivor an Accord row on a fetch-failed page, left untouched by design and excluded from the median anyway — and roughly tripled sale rows: M550i 9→45, 750i 6→33, Atlas 6→20, CR-V 0→14, Accord 0→16, Civic 0→19. The relabor sweep (via `18a722d`) put `repairpal_motor` observations on all ten configs — own nameplate for six, sibling-resolved for the M550i pair — and along the way exposed and fixed a `runQuery` undefined→null serialization bug that broke sibling resolution for chassis-code-less cars like the Atlases (`1c0cc54`). The data-good report shows every config at **N−1/N quote-grade with one shared gap: `rotor_replacement`** — RepairPal's rotor page exists but Firecrawl 500s on it, so rotors quote via tier_estimate meanwhile. The director panel was verified live end-to-end with Waleed's token: reprice, re-enrich (the Civic completed from the UI), backfill parts, and mark-verified, every audit row attributed server-side from the session (`8ca5fb3`). Status docs threaded through the run (`38b279e`, `14263df`, `63ab010`, `b5beeef`), closing with the final scoreboard (`5c789e3`): **750i and both M550i configs 25/25, both Jettas 28/30** (timing belt and water pump priced at last, on a belt basis), **Civic 24/25, CR-V and Atlas 2.0T 23/25, Atlas V6 22/25, Accord 23/30** — the remaining gaps structural (O-ring fallback, timing-kit discovery, old-Honda legacy fitments), not batch-fixable.

## Chapter 10 — In-app Oto grows up: beta fixes, the Sim tab, and a faithful sim (Jun 8 – early Jun 9)

The era closes on the in-app assistant. Director configs gained per-config backfill buttons — re-enrich / parts / reprice (`98bf93f`). The director panel's audit EntityPreview crash on non-id entity_ids was fixed (`f795292`), and the Oto Sim tab let directors chat with in-app Oto as any user — user → car → chat (`70910a8`).

The first hours of Jun 9 finished the thought: sim conversations got vehicles attached so Oto sees the user's car (`1418f90`), then the sim became *faithful* — threading the acting user through Oto's actual tool data-reads (`ea63f4f`). A session handoff was written (`d4a7527`), `repriceConfigParts` was made fire-and-return with the scrape moved to a scheduled action (`ccf0206`), and the handoff marked it done (`0183a60`) — the last commit before the labor-time RepairPal spec (`2d828d2`) opened the next era.

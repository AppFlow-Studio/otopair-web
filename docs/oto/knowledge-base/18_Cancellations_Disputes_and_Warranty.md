# Cancellations, Disputes and Warranty

## Overview

What happens when a booking doesn't go to plan: cancelling, rescheduling, no-shows, a shop that never answers, a problem after the job, and who stands behind the repair. These are the default rules the app enforces across the network today. The exact windows for a booking are shown on that booking before the driver confirms, and again when they cancel — if this ever differs from the app, the app's numbers are the ones enforced. The site's "Cancellations and No-Shows" page (otopair.com/cancellation) and "Warranties and Service Issues" page (otopair.com/warranties) summarize them; the Terms of Use govern.

## Cancelling

- **The $20 hold is a hold, not a charge** — show up, or cancel 24 hours or more before, and it's never charged
- **24 hours or more before the appointment** — free; the $20 hold is released in full
- **Inside 24 hours** — the $20 hold is kept as a late-cancellation fee. It's capped at the hold: never more than $20
- The booking screen shows whether cancelling right now is free, and exactly what the fee would be, before the driver confirms
- **Inside 24 hours, but the shop is fine with it** — message the shop. A cancellation the shop makes on its side releases the hold in full
- **Car already at the shop** — cancelling becomes a request: ask for pickup through the booking, and the shop brings the car out and cancels on its side. Once work has started, cancellation isn't available in the app: message the shop directly, and the driver is responsible for authorized work done up to that point

## Rescheduling

- Free up to 12 hours before the appointment, up to 2 times per booking. The $20 hold carries over to the new time
- A reschedule goes back to the shop to re-confirm the new slot
- Inside 12 hours, or after the second reschedule, message the shop — it can move the appointment on its side

## No-shows

- If the driver doesn't arrive for a confirmed appointment, the shop may mark a no-show, and the $20 hold is forfeited — that's the whole fee, nothing more
- The app reminds the driver first, so a delay can become a message to the shop instead

## When the hold is released in full

- The shop cancels the booking
- The shop never accepts the request — it expires after 48 hours, or 2 hours past the requested appointment time, whichever comes first, and the app invites the driver to rebook

## The one charge people don't expect

If the shop's estimate after inspection — before any work starts — is above what the driver approved and they don't answer within 24 hours, the estimate expires and is treated as declined. The $20 hold is kept to pay the shop for the inspection it already did.

That rule is only for the estimate before work starts. A request to add work during the job is different: left unanswered for 24 hours, the job continues at the last price the driver approved, and the added work isn't charged. Nothing is kept.

## If something goes wrong with a job

1. **Message the shop first**, from the booking. Most problems are a conversation, not a case, and the thread keeps a record
2. **If that doesn't settle it, tell Otopair in the app within 7 days of the service** — by opening a dispute from the booking, once the final amount has been charged. That 7-day window is for Otopair's help; it doesn't limit the driver's rights against the shop, under its warranty, or under the law. One dispute can be open on a booking at a time
3. **Pick a reason** — the wrong part was fitted, overcharged against what was approved, work paid for wasn't done, declined work was charged anyway, a concern about quality, or something else — and add notes
4. **A person at Otopair reviews** the job record: the price approved, every approval request and the answer, the messages on both sides and the itemized receipt. Otopair can hold back or reverse the shop's payout while it reviews
5. **The outcome** is no refund, a partial refund or a full refund. It's a review, not an automatic reversal

## Refunds

- A refund is issued by the shop through Otopair, back to the card the driver paid with — in full or in part. It isn't automatic on the decision
- Refunds usually reach the card within 5–10 business days; the bank controls the last step
- Charged in error? Tell Otopair in the app
- Cash paid at a walk-in has nothing to reverse on a card, so it's refunded in person by the shop
- If a driver takes a charge up with their card issuer instead, that follows the card network's process, and the shop responds to it through Stripe

## Who stands behind the repair

- Otopair does not warrant repairs. The shop performs the work and stands behind it
- Otopair is a marketplace: it isn't a repair shop, doesn't employ the mechanics and doesn't take custody of the car
- Any parts or labor warranty is the shop's own, on the shop's terms — it differs from shop to shop and by job, and some shops offer none. Ask the shop about its warranty before booking; asked in the app, the answer stays on the record
- What Otopair provides is not a warranty: a written estimate for every booking, a record of every service, and help if something goes wrong
- Under New York law, a shop that has given a written estimate can't charge more than it without the driver's consent. Every Otopair booking is a written estimate, and additional work needs the driver's approval in the app first — Otopair records the moment it was given
- Otopair keeps the paper trail: the price approved, every estimate and answer, the messages with the shop, and the itemized receipt
- A warranty claim is between the driver and the shop, under the shop's terms. Support can share the job record if it helps

## How to answer common questions

**"Can I cancel?"**
"Yes, from the booking in the app. It's free up to 24 hours before your appointment. Inside 24 hours, the $20 hold is kept as a late-cancellation fee — never more than $20. The app shows you before you confirm."

**"If I cancel the morning of, will I be charged?"**
"That's inside 24 hours, so the $20 hold is kept as a late-cancellation fee — and that's the most it can be. If the shop's fine with it, message them: a cancellation the shop makes releases your hold in full."

**"What if I don't show up?"**
"The shop can mark it as a no-show, and the $20 hold is kept. That's the whole fee — nothing more."

**"Can I reschedule?"**
"Yes — free up to 12 hours before, up to twice per booking, and your hold just moves to the new time. After that, message the shop."

**"The shop found more work — what if I don't answer?"**
"It depends on when. If the shop asks to add work during the job, nothing extra happens without your yes: after 24 hours the job continues at the last price you approved, and the added work isn't charged. The one exception is the shop's estimate after it inspects your car, before work starts — if that's above what you approved and you don't answer within 24 hours, it's treated as declined and the $20 hold is kept to pay for the inspection."

**"What if the shop cancels, or never answers?"**
"Then your $20 hold is released in full and nothing is charged."

**"How do refunds work?"**
"Message the shop from your booking first — most things get sorted there. If not, tell us in the app within 7 days of the service by opening a dispute from the booking. A person at Otopair reviews the job record and the messages on both sides and decides: no refund, a partial refund or a full refund. Any refund goes back to the card you paid with, usually within 5–10 business days."

**"Does Otopair guarantee the repair?"**
"No — Otopair doesn't warrant repairs. The shop does the work and stands behind it on its own terms. Otopair locks the price, keeps the full record of the job and reviews disputes opened from the booking in the app."

**"What warranty do I get?"**
"That's the shop's warranty, and it varies by shop and by job. Ask the shop in the app so the answer is on the record."

**"The part failed a month later — who do I contact?"**
"The shop first, through the app — a part failing later is a question for the shop's own warranty, and your itemized receipt is the record of what was done. Otopair's window to help is 7 days from the service, but that doesn't limit your rights against the shop, under its warranty or under the law. The Warranties and Service Issues page on the site explains it."

## Important boundaries

- Do **not** say cancelling is always free, that the $20 is never kept, or that the rules vary by shop — the defaults apply across the network today
- Do **not** say a cancellation or no-show fee can be more than $20
- Do **not** say refunds are automatic, that Otopair itself pays them, or that the driver doesn't need to contact the shop
- Do **not** promise dispute outcomes or a refund date — refunds usually take 5–10 business days, and the bank controls the last step
- Do **not** say Otopair warrants, guarantees or insures a repair, do **not** invent warranty lengths or terms, and never use the words "guarantee", "covered" or "make it right" about Otopair
- Do **not** say a failed part is "usually covered" or that the shop's warranty will cover it — warranties vary by shop and by job, and some shops offer none
- Do **not** say a shop's warranty terms show on its listing — drivers ask the shop about its warranty before booking
- Do **not** interpret the terms of service, liability limits or warranty law — point to the site's Cancellations and No-Shows and Warranties and Service Issues pages; the Terms of Use govern
- Oto can't open a dispute or a ticket for anyone — disputes are opened from the booking in the app
